Maintenance Guidelines

  • Never let waterproof India ink dry completely inside technical dip pens.
  • Rinse metal nibs in lukewarm water and wipe dry with a lint-free cloth.
  • Store pigment fineliners horizontally to ensure balanced capillary flow.

Inking represents one of the most definitive disciplines in graphic art. Unlike graphite or charcoal, which allow for progressive erasure and modification, an ink stroke commits with immediate authority. Modern disposable technical pens utilize micro-pigment particles suspended in water-based binders.

Because pigment particles do not bleed into the paper fibers like dye-based inks, they produce razor-sharp edges and offer exceptional resistance against UV sunlight fading and water solubility once cured. Traditional India ink pairs pure carbon black with shellac, creating an indelible semi-gloss sheen that resists watercolor washes.

For artists seeking dynamic variation in stroke width, the traditional dip pen remains unparalleled. By varying downward hand pressure, the dual tines of a flexible steel nib separate, expanding line weight from hairline subtleties to bold strokes in a continuous, flowing sweep.

Beyond pens and inkwells, a well-equipped studio requires dedicated hardware: an inclined drawing board to avoid perspective distortion, beveled stainless steel rulers that prevent ink from bleeding underneath the guide edge, and low-tack artist drafting tape that holds paper securely without lifting the sizing or tearing borders upon removal.
