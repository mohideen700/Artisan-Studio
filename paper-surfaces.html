The ground upon which an artist draws is far more than a passive backdrop; its fiber chemistry, surface tooth, and density determine how pigment binds and refracts ambient light. Paper weight is universally quantified in grams per square meter (gsm). Lightweight sketchbook paper generally ranges between 80 and 100 gsm, suitable for swift exploratory doodles and preliminary sketches.

For intensive finished pieces, artists favor heavy cartridge paper (150 to 220 gsm) or heavyweight boards (250 to 300+ gsm). Heavy sheets withstand rigorous erasing, blending stumps, and the moisture inherent in multi-media washes without warping, buckling, or surface pilling.

Surface texture presents another critical variable. Plate smooth Bristol board offers a polished surface that allows technical drafting pens and hard graphite leads to glide effortlessly with microscopic precision. In contrast, vellum or cold-pressed sheets possess a distinct tactile grain.

Toned papers (warm tan or cool slate grey) fundamentally shift an artist's working methodology. By initiating work on a mid-value background, the artist does not begin from extreme white; instead, shadows are rendered with deep graphite or charcoal, while highlights are established using white chalk, expanding the overall dynamic range.
